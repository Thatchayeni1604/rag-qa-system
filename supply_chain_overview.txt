Supply Chain & Logistics — Key Concepts
=======================================

YARD MANAGEMENT
Yard management refers to the coordination and tracking of trucks, trailers, and containers
within a facility's yard or staging area. A Yard Management System (YMS) helps warehouses
track assets in real time, reduce detention costs, and ensure dock doors are efficiently utilized.
Key metrics: dwell time, gate turnaround time, dock utilization rate.

TERMINAL THROUGHPUT
Terminal throughput is the volume of cargo or containers processed by a terminal (port, rail, or
warehouse) within a given time period. High throughput indicates efficient operations. Throughput
is affected by equipment availability, labor efficiency, berth occupancy, and cargo dwell time.
Improving terminal throughput is a core goal of supply chain AI systems like Kaleris.

DEMAND FORECASTING IN SUPPLY CHAINS
AI-based demand forecasting uses historical data, seasonal patterns, and external signals
(weather, market trends) to predict future inventory needs. Accurate forecasting reduces
overstock, prevents stockouts, and lowers carrying costs. Common models: ARIMA, SARIMA,
LSTM, XGBoost.

LAST-MILE DELIVERY
The final step of the delivery process from a distribution hub to the end customer. This is
typically the most expensive and time-consuming segment. AI optimizes routing, reduces
failed deliveries, and improves ETA prediction.

INTERMODAL LOGISTICS
Use of multiple modes of transport (ship, rail, truck) for a single shipment. AI platforms
help coordinate handoffs, track containers across modes, and predict delays.

VECTOR DATABASE IN SUPPLY CHAIN AI
Vector databases like ChromaDB and Pinecone store embeddings of logistics documents,
SOPs, regulations, and shipping records. They enable semantic search, allowing AI agents
to retrieve relevant context and answer queries about shipment status, compliance rules,
or carrier performance.

GENERATIVE AI IN SUPPLY CHAINS
LLMs and GenAI agents are increasingly used to automate exception management, generate
shipment summaries, draft supplier communications, and build conversational interfaces
on top of logistics data platforms.

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
RAG combines a retriever (vector database) with an LLM generator. For supply chains,
this means an agent can answer "what is the current dwell time SLA for this carrier?"
by retrieving relevant SLA documents from a vector store and generating a precise answer.
